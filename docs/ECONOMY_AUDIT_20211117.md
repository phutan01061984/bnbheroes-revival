# BNB HEROES Economy Audit — 17 Nov 2021 preservation snapshot

**Target:** BSC block `12,730,607` — `2021-11-17T19:08:02Z`, one second after the preserved frontend timestamp.

This report analyzes the restored game as an economic machine. It is a preservation/research document, not investment advice and not a claim about realized player returns.

## Executive picture

```text
PLAYER
  │ buys/holds BNBH
  ▼
GAME OPERATIONS ── Recruit / Expedite / Town / Unlock / Market fee
  │ spends BNBH
  ▼
CORE / TOKEN ECONOMY
  ├──> burn / liquidity                     (BNBH sinks)
  └──> accumulated BNBH sold through AMM
             │
             ▼
            BNB
             │
             ▼
         BNBH POOL  ────────────────┐
                                    │ claim
FIGHT ──> pending BNB liability ────┴──> PLAYER receives BNB
```

The central design fact is the currency mismatch:

- **Player progression spends BNBH.**
- **Combat rewards accrue and claim in BNB.**

Therefore sustainability is controlled by whether the token/game mechanism can refill the BNB pool at least as fast as players create and claim BNB liabilities.

## Fight engine: how BNB liability is created

For a Hero with effective Attack `A`, Armor `D`, Speed `S`:

```text
threshold        = baseChance + floor(A / 10)
p(win)           = (threshold - 1) / 1000     # seed 0..999, strict > 1000
BNB if win       = baseBNB × (1 + S/1000) × BankMultiplier
HP loss if win   = requiredHP - floor(D/10)
HP loss if fail  = requiredHP
HP regenerated   = 1 HP / 86 sec = 1004.65 HP/day
```

Fight itself does not immediately send BNB. It increments the player's pending `balances`. `claimRewards()` later asks the BNBHPool to send real BNB.

## Average newly recruited Hero

Weighting all 21 templates by the exact 100-entry recruit table:

- average Boss output, base Town: **0.045077 BNB/day gross**;
- average best Tier-1 output, base Town: **0.026969 BNB/day gross**;
- average Boss output with Bank L4 + Training L4: **0.059929 BNB/day gross**.

Using same-block Oracle value, Recruit costs exactly **0.300 BNB-equivalent**. Simple mechanical payback is therefore:

```text
0.300 / 0.045077 ≈ 6.66 days gross
```

If claiming at the earliest 20% tax rate, the equivalent simple payback is about **8.32 days**. Adding Expedite increases entry cost to 0.33 BNB-equivalent and gross simple payback to about **7.32 days**.

These are *mechanical expectation ratios*, not historical realized ROI: they ignore gas, downtime, behavior, price impact, claim batching, pool refill constraints and token-price movement.

## Claim tax = throttle, not permanent economic sink

The first positive reward starts a 48-hour lock. At the first eligible instant after the lock, tax is still 20%; it then falls 2 percentage points per elapsed day after unlock and reaches 0 after 10 more days.

```text
first reward   +48h       +5d after unlock          +10d after unlock
    │            │                │                        │
    ├── LOCK ────┼── 20% ──18% ... 12% ... ───────────── 0%
```

If players wait, the tax eventually stops reducing pool outflow. It mainly delays claims and gives the pool/refill mechanism more time.

## Town economy

- **Bank L4:** +16% BNB reward — directly increases BNB liabilities.
- **Training L4:** +90 Attack/Armor/Speed — increases chance/reward efficiency and reduces expected HP loss.
- **Barracks L4:** +90 XP on wins — accelerates XP/stat progression.
- **Inn:** the 17-Nov implementation has the known missing-assignment bug; HP regeneration remains 86 sec/HP despite Inn upgrades.

Thus Town is simultaneously a BNBH sink and, especially through Bank/Training, a multiplier on future BNB extraction capacity.

## Where BNBHPool funding comes from

This part has two evidence levels.

### Exact 17-Nov facts

At the target block:

- BNBH token charges **11% BNB fee + 1% liquidity fee** on fee-bearing AMM trades.
- Token's `bnbPoolAddress` equals the game's BNBHPool.
- Core's `bnbhPool` equals the same pool.
- Pool authorizes the Core address to call `claimBNB`.
- Core `dividePercent = 70`.
- Pool contains **3,270.93 BNB**.

### Architecture corroborated by recovered late-Nov Solidity mirror

The surviving mirror shows:

1. game operations `transferFrom` BNBH into Core;
2. when the operation-balance threshold is reached, `dividePercent` of that batch goes to the BNBH token contract and the remainder to the configured burn address;
3. BNBH trading fees also accumulate in the token contract;
4. token `swapAndCharge()` sells accumulated BNBH through PancakeSwap for BNB, allocates a liquidity portion, and sends remaining BNB to `bnbPoolAddress`;
5. Core `claimRewards()` calls `bnbhPool.claimBNB(player, amount)`.

This is the critical feedback loop:

```text
More BNBH spending/trading
        ↓
more BNBH accumulated for swap
        ↓
BNBH sell pressure → BNB
        ↓
BNBHPool refill
        ↓
funds BNB reward claims
```

The source mirror is later than the target snapshot, so its exact batch threshold/internal implementation is **corroboration**, not mislabeled byte-identical 17-Nov source. The key fee/split/address state above is direct 17-Nov chain evidence.

## Pool stress test

At the snapshot there were 35,451 Hero NFTs and 3,270.93 BNB in the reward pool. If an active Hero behaves like the recruit-weighted average and farms Boss at base Town, gross reward capacity is ~0.045077 BNB/day/Hero.

With **zero new pool inflow**:

| Active share of minted Heroes | Active Heroes | Gross BNB/day | Pool runway |
|---:|---:|---:|---:|
| 1% | 355 | 15.98 | 204.7 d |
| 5% | 1,773 | 79.90 | 40.9 d |
| 10% | 3,545 | 159.80 | 20.5 d |
| 25% | 8,863 | 399.51 | 8.2 d |
| 50% | 17,726 | 799.01 | 4.1 d |
| 100% | 35,451 | 1,598.02 | **2.05 d** |

This is a **stress test, not a historical depletion forecast**. Real inflow from BNBH trading/game operations refilled the pool, many Heroes were not continuously active, and claim tax/timing altered realized outflow.

The useful conclusion is the scale mismatch: at only 10% theoretical Boss utilization, maintaining the pool indefinitely would require roughly **160 BNB/day** of net refill before considering claim-tax reduction.

## Why growth can look spectacular

1. Recruit is a rarity lottery, creating repeat demand.
2. The entry cost is one-off but the Hero is a reusable BNB-producing asset.
3. Very short mechanical payback makes reinvestment rational while liquidity is healthy.
4. Town upgrades turn more BNBH spend into higher future output.
5. Marketability gives Hero NFTs a resale/speculation narrative.
6. 48h claim lock suppresses immediate visible outflow.
7. BNBH trading itself helps refill the BNB reward pool, so price/volume growth can temporarily reinforce gameplay rewards.

## Why the structure is reflexive and fragile

The game is sustainable only while:

```text
BNB pool refill/day >= BNB actually claimed/day
```

But each new Recruit has two opposing effects:

- positive now: BNBH demand / operation spend / burn-swap contribution;
- negative later: one more reusable Hero capable of generating BNB liabilities indefinitely.

If BNBH price/volume/user spending slows while the installed Hero base keeps fighting, the refill side can shrink faster than payout capacity. Because reward values are BNB-denominated, a falling BNBH price makes progression inputs cheaper in BNB terms unless Oracle repricing compensates quickly, while existing BNB reward liabilities remain in BNB.

That is a **reflexive play-to-earn loop**. It becomes Ponzi-like *if* continued rewards materially require liquidity/value from later token buyers/players rather than independent external revenue. The recovered contracts establish the token-trading/game-spend → BNBHPool feedback loop; they do not by themselves prove every historical dollar entering the pool came from new entrants, so the stronger accusation should not be stated as an unconditional fact.
